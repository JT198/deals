How Deal Finder works

What it does

It watches Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist within your radius (Setup tab) for 4-seat and 2-seat UTVs, ATVs, 3-wheelers, jet skis, snowmobiles, zero-turn mowers and trailers. It reads every ad, works out what similar machines usually list for, and flags the ones priced well below that. Private sellers are the focus; dealers are shown but marked.

Trailers

  • Every trailer gets a fits a 4-seat UTV check: open deck 14 ft+ (16 ideal), 7 ft wide class (~80 in between fenders; UTVs are 62-64 in), tandem axle or 5,000 lb+ GVWR, and ~7 ft inside if enclosed. "May fit" means one of those isn't stated or is borderline - ask the seller.
  • New-price check (trailers): the finder knows what new trailers of the same type, size and weight class list for at dealers. A used one's typical price is capped at 85% of that, and a used trailer priced within 10% of new is flagged priced like a new one and can't score above 50. It only sees dealers who post on Marketplace or Craigslist, so check a dealer's own site for sale prices before offering.
  • Trailers are compared by type, weight class (7K / 10K / 14K+), body style (between the fenders vs deckover) and size (length within 2 ft, same axle count), or per foot when there aren't enough the same size. Most trailers on Marketplace are new dealer stock - set Condition to "used only" to hide them.
  • Alerts: trailers that fit a UTV alert normally; anything else only when it's an exceptional deal (score 85+). Just-listed alerts are for UTV-size trailers only. Tick Fits a 4-seat UTV in the filters to see just those.
  • Trailers whose loaded rating (GVWR) is above your truck's towing capacity (Setup; default 12,700 lb for the F-150 PowerBoost) get a warning tag - fine if you never load them near capacity.
  • Before buying: title and VIN plate match, tire date codes, bearings, floor, lights, brakes (brakes need a brake controller in your truck), and that its loaded weight is within your truck's towing capacity.

How often it looks

  • Full scan every 20 minutes, 6am-11pm: all searches on Craigslist; on Facebook about 32 searches per scan, most-overdue first - 4-seat UTV, mower and trailer searches roughly every 40 minutes, ATVs, 2-seat UTVs and 3-wheelers hourly, jet skis and snowmobiles every 90 minutes.
  • Facebook route (Setup): the finder reaches Facebook from your home IP by default. If you add a VPN provider's proxy, "Auto" switches Facebook traffic to it while the home IP is blocked and back when the block lifts; Craigslist and everything else always use the home network. The header says when Facebook is paused or going via the proxy.
  • ⚡ Fast lane every 10 minutes: only the searches marked ⚡ in Setup, newest first, so a new listing usually shows up 10-20 minutes after it's posted.
  • 🔎 Deep sweep every 10 minutes, day and night, a few searches at a time: Facebook only shows a search's first couple dozen listings, so the scans above mostly see what was posted recently. The sweep re-runs every search one price range at a time (splitting a range while it keeps turning up listings we didn't have) to reach older listings and anything the other scans missed. Its first pass digs up everything posted before the finder started - those older listings don't send alerts, they just show up here, and you get one Telegram summary when it's done. A full pass takes a few days; Setup shows how far along it is.
  • For each new ad it opens the listing, then a local AI model reads it and pulls out the year, model, miles/hours, deck size, add-ons, whether it's a dealer, and any problems (red flags). Parts, "wanted" ads and the wrong kind of machine are dropped.

"Typical" price

  • The middle asking price of the same model family (e.g. RZR XP 4, Toro TimeCutter) seen in the last 6 months, within a model year either side, adjusted about 8% per year of age. Mowers are matched on deck size when there are enough; snowmobiles on track length (within 9") and engine size (within 150cc). Jet skis sold as a pair are priced per ski - "typical" for the pair is two skis plus one trailer. Vintage 3-wheelers don't lose value with age, so no age adjustment for those.
  • Adjusted for use: when the ad states miles or hours, similar machines are lined up to the same use before taking the middle price. How much a mile or an hour is worth is learned from the listings for that model, starting from a sensible default (about 2.5% per 1,000 miles for UTVs, 4% per 1,000 miles for snowmobiles, 6% per 100 hours for jet skis, 4% per 100 hours for mowers). The grey line under "Typical" shows the adjustment.
  • ⚠ Miles unclear: ads say things like "clutches replaced 60 miles ago" - that's not the odometer. When the only number is "since a repair", or the mileage is implausibly low for the machine's age and not clearly stated, the finder ignores it for pricing and tells you to ask.
  • Adjusted for equipment: cab, heat and A/C (UTVs) are worth a percentage learned from the listings (a full cab with heat and A/C is currently worth ~25%); a plow adds ~$600 and an included trailer ~$1,500 (~$900 with a jet ski, ~$1,200 with a snowmobile). So a NorthStar isn't compared straight against open machines. The grey line shows these adjustments too.
  • Adjusted for trim in the families where trim moves the price on its own: Maverick X3 / X3 MAX (DS = lower, X ds / X rs / X rc / X mr = top, RS and Turbo RR in between), RZR Pro XP 4 (Sport = lower, Ultimate = top), RZR XP (Turbo = top) and Commander (XT / DPS = lower, XT-P / X mr = top). Read from the title and the start of the ad. Worth a percentage learned from the listings (currently about −9% for a lower trim, +7% for a top trim). Ads that don't say the trim are treated as the middle.
  • Few same-year comps: when only a handful of listings are within a model year, their median is blended with the model's overall price-by-year trend (the fewer comps, the more weight on the trend, never more than 12%), so a few cheap or loaded listings can't price a 2022 below a 2020 of the same model. Not used for trailers, vintage machines, "Other" groups or machines over 12 years old. The grey line says when it happened.
  • Trailers with too few same-size comparisons are priced per foot from trailers of the same type.
  • Typically sells around: the same calculation run on listings marked sold on Facebook (pulled three times a day with Facebook's Sold filter). When a model has too few sold listings, it's estimated from how far sold prices sit under asking across the category. Offers anchor on this when it's there - it's what similar ones actually went for. Facebook only shows the last listed price of a sold item, so when similar ones sold at about asking the card just says ✓ Selling at about asking - a sign sellers are getting their price and there's less room to haggle.
  • "Typical asking" uses asking prices - sold prices aren't public - so "typical" means what these usually list for. With few listings of a model the number is rougher; the comps count tells you how many it's based on.
  • New/dealer-stock units get no typical price (used comps don't apply); compare those to MSRP.

Score (the number on each card)

  • 50 is a normally priced listing. 60-74 "Good", 75+ "Great".
  • Goes up for being under typical, price cuts, and signs the seller is motivated (OBO, must sell). Goes down for dealers, new stock, and being over typical.
  • Anything with red flags (needs work, title problems, scam signs) is capped at 70 - never "Great".
  • Prices under $100 or under 20% of typical are treated as placeholders ("$1 - make an offer"), not deals.
  • Few comps or no model year pull the score toward the middle, because the typical price is less certain.

Offer guidance

  • Walk away above - the typical price for this machine (or the asking price, if lower). Paying more means you're paying above market.
  • Aim to pay - normally 7% under that, more when the seller is motivated, it's a dealer, or it has sat for 3+ weeks; less when it was just listed (other buyers are coming).
  • Open with - a little under your aim, never below 75% of asking.
  • If it's already 15%+ under typical, the guidance flips: offer close to asking and move fast - lowballing a real deal usually loses it.
  • Red flags: get a repair estimate and take it off these numbers. Always check the title/VIN in person.

Telegram alerts

  • Map (Cards / Map toggle on Best deals): the listings your filters select, colored by score. Craigslist listings sit at their exact map pin; Facebook only gives the town, so those sit at the town center (spread a little). Tap Great / Good / Other above the map to hide or show that group (remembered on this device). 📍 My location puts a pulsing blue dot where you are (your browser asks permission once) and adds "N mi from you" to each pin's popup; tap it again to stop. Only works on https://deals.alerolabs.ai, not the LAN address.
  • Deal alerts - private listings scoring at or above the threshold in Setup, and - if you set the savings floors - at least that % and that many dollars under typical (or under what similar ones sell for, when lower). Sent again if the score jumps 10+ points (e.g. a price cut).
  • 🆕 Just-listed alerts - fresh private listings (posted within 2 hours) that are priced normally or better and have no red flags, so you can be first to message. On for 4-seat UTVs, mowers and UTV-size trailers; change per category in Setup.
  • ⭐ Watch alerts - for starred listings: price drops, pending, sold/removed, back on the market. Stars are shared, so Alex's stars notify you too.
  • Per category you can turn each alert type on or off and set a max price and minimum year. Listings far outside the radius and cross-posts of something already sent are skipped.

Using the dashboard

  • Category bar picks what you're looking at; model chips narrow it to specific families.
  • "Listed in the last hour" / "new since your last visit" in the header are quick views - click to see just those, click again (or ✕) to go back. Your filters aren't changed.
  • NEW marks listings added since your last visit in this browser; Just listed = posted within a day.
  • Listings are re-checked to catch removals: anything scoring 60+ every 3 hours, the rest about daily. When a listing fails to load it's tagged ⚠ May be sold/removed, sinks in the list, and is re-checked every scan - three misses in a row and it's gone (usually within an hour). The Gone button removes one right away.
  • Star to watch one: it's re-checked every scan and Telegram tells you when the price drops, it goes pending, or it's sold/removed. Hide to get it out of the way (Show hidden brings them back). Stars and hides are shared by everyone who uses this dashboard.
  • Market tab: every asking price seen for a model by year, a weekly market pulse (new listings, asking vs typical, how fast things go), and recently sold or removed listings with their last asking price - the closest thing to sold prices.
  • Note on a card: a shared note for you and Alex ("messaged seller, wants $14k firm"). Notes are searchable.
  • Search looks through titles, the full ad text, the AI summary, add-ons and notes.
  • Morning digest at 7:00 on Telegram: the best new private listings per category from the last day, your watched listings with any price drops, and how the market is pricing.
  • Scorecard (Market tab, bottom): whether high scores really sell faster than low ones, what the pricing has learned from the listings, and a day-by-day history so you can see if it's improving.
  • Copy message (inside Offer guidance): a ready opener for the seller with your offer - edit, copy, paste into Messenger.
  • Sell mine tab: describe your machine and get a list price, what to expect, a quick-sale price, the closest listings, and a drafted ad (written by the local AI from only what you enter).
  • Setup: radius and home ZIP, alert rules, searches (add your own; ⚡ puts one in the fast lane), and recent scan history with any problems.

Limits worth knowing

  • Facebook is read without an account. If Facebook starts blocking it, Setup → Recent scans will say so.
  • OfferUp and ATVTrader aren't covered (they block this kind of reading).
  • The AI occasionally misreads an ad (wrong model family, 2-seat vs 4-seat). The card summary and the listing itself are the final word.
